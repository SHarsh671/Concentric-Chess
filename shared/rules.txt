General assumptions:
- All moves can be captures unless otherwise specified.

Pawn:
- Moves across one edge in the forward direction, first move can be two edges.
- Captures along forward vertices.
Berolina pawn (pawns when in the center of the board):
- Moves across one vertex in the forward direction, first move can be two vertices.
- Captures along forward edges, once past the middle along adjacent edges.
Bishop:
- Moves any number of spaces across a vertex.
- Can move across an edge or vertex once to a cell of the opposite color.
Rook:
- Moves any number of spaces across an edge.
Knight:
- Moves across an edge and then across a vertex, or across a vertex and then across an edge, so long as the final cell is not a neighbor of the stating cell.
Queen:
- Moves as either a bishop or a rook.
King:
- Moves across one edge or vertex in any direction.
- First move may leap two cells in any direction, so long as the intermediary cell is not threatened.
  - May also make the knight's move as a first move, so long as the intermediary cell is not threatened.
- Cannot move or capture if it would put the king in check.
Prince:
- Moves across one edge or vertex in any direction.
- First move may move two cells forward, but may not capture or leap.

En passant:
- If a pawn moves two spaces forward, all enemy pawns can capture it as if it had only moved a single space.

Archimedean updates:
- Pawns on the two far columns may move across a sideways edge when they reach the end of their spirals.

Draw conditions:
- If the same board state occurs three times.
- 50 moves pass without a capture or a pawn move.
- Insufficient material to checkmate (king and king, king and one bishop, king and one knight, king and two bishops on same color without color switch move, others are possible but too complicated for right now).
- If a player is not in check but has no legal moves, the game is a stalemate.

Check and checkmate:
- A king is in check if it is threatened by an enemy piece.
- A king is in checkmate if it is in check and no legal move by any piece results in a state where the king is not in check.
- Any move that would result in the king being in check is illegal (i.e. capturing one threatening piece but exposing another).
